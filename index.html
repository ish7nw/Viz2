Arrivals, origins and settlement, 1788–2025

Data: Australian Bureau of Statistics (country of birth, June 2025; 2021 state estimates). Built with Vega-Lite.

01Migration over time

Figure 1 shows the overseas-born share of the population from the first country-of-birth records in 1891. Three phases stand out: a long decline from 1891 to 1947, through Federation, the Immigration Restriction Act, two world wars and the Depression; a steep rise after 1947 under the post-war migration program; and a second rise from 2000, interrupted briefly by the COVID-19 border closure in 2020–21.

02Arrival

The First Fleet of eleven ships left Portsmouth on 13 May 1787 with more than 1,500 people and reached Botany Bay in January 1788, after stops at Tenerife, Rio de Janeiro and Cape Town (Figure 2). About 162,000 convicts were transported to Australia between 1788 and 1868.

03Countries of origin

3.1Where residents were born

Figure 3 places the ten largest overseas birthplaces on a map centred on Sydney. Rings mark equal distance from Sydney every 2,500 km, so each country can be read against how far it is from Australia. New Zealand is the nearest large source at about 2,200 km. India and China lie at 9,000–10,000 km, and England lies beyond 15,000 km.

3.2Flows

Figure 4 draws a line from each birthplace to Australia, with line width proportional to the number of residents in 2025. The three widest flows, from India, England and China, together account for 2.67 million people, about 30% of the overseas-born population.

3.3Change, 2015–2025

Between 2015 and 2025 the India-born population grew by 522,000, the largest increase of any country, followed by China (223,000), the Philippines (171,000) and Nepal (163,000). England is the only top-ten birthplace that declined, by about 36,000, reflecting the ageing of the post-war cohort (Figure 5).

4.1Share born overseas by region

Below the state level, the overseas-born population is concentrated in the capital cities (Figure 6). In 2021, 34.4% of residents of the Greater Capital City areas were born overseas, against 14.5% in the rest of the country. Sydney – Parramatta had the highest share of the 88 SA4 regions, 52.2%, followed by Sydney – Ryde (48.0%) and Sydney – South West (47.4%). The lowest shares were in inland New South Wales, led by Far West and Orana at 7.3%. Outside the capitals, only the Gold Coast came close to the national figure, at 28.9%.

4.2Level and growth

Figure 8 combines the 2021 share with its change since 2011. Fifteen regions are high on both measures, all of them in the capital cities: eight in Sydney, four in Melbourne, two in Brisbane and one in Perth. Sydney – Ryde grew the most, by 7.9 percentage points, to 48.0%. Most of regional Australia is low on both measures, and in six regions the share did not rise at all, including Illawarra and the Mornington Peninsula.

4.3Where overseas-born residents live

Figures 9 to 11 show the same 2021 totals in three ways. The dot density map shows concentration in the south-east. The cartogram resizes each state by its overseas-born population, and the pie charts compare overseas-born and Australian-born residents within each state.

4.4Largest communities

The largest overseas birthplace differs by state: China in New South Wales, India in Victoria and the ACT, New Zealand in Queensland, the Philippines in the Northern Territory, and England in Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania (Figure 12).
